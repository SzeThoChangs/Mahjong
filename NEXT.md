# Where we left off — 2026-09-05

Read this first. `PLAN.md` is the project. `FINDINGS.md` is everything we measured and why.

## The coach got its second ever change, and it is the value side

`solver/src/tables.ts` turns a hand into a number of chips. Re-fitting it on our own 150,000 hands
was the first job in the old NEXT. It is done, and the answer has two halves.

**Replacing the study's numbers with ours loses.** Four scales, 32,000 paired deals each, every one
behind the coach from -0.832 to -1.093 chips a game, worse the more of our numbers it used. The
fitted coach wins as often and wins smaller: half its wins at the table minimum against the coach's
four in ten, half as many half-colour hands, and a deal-in rate that does not move. It is the
`onlycheap` arm in milder form.

**The reason is the estimator, so do not simply try again with more data.** `valuefit.ts` averages
what a seat finally won over every decision where a plan was on top, which is the value of the
POSITION under a coach that switches plans. The table is asked what a plan is worth if you play for
it. The two come apart in proportion to how often the coach abandons the plan, so the cheap hand
keeps its spread and a late half-colour hand loses four fifths of it. Anyone re-opening this has to
fit the value of COMMITTING, which needs hands generated by bots that pick a plan and keep it.

**What shipped is one constant per table row.** Inside a plan our hands reproduce the study's
ordering at an R-squared of 0.86 to 1.00, so there was nothing to improve there. Between plans they
disagree, and the rows want scales from 1.86 to 5.51 - which is why the global `--scale` sweep could
never have settled it. `datagen/src/valuerows.ts` measures those slopes and keeps the study's own
numbers, changing only the weight of one plan against another. Worth **+0.200 +/- 0.041 chips a game
over 64,000 paired deals on eight ranges named in advance, positive on all eight**, against a
matched flat multiplier that loses 0.163. `solver/src/tools/baketables.ts` bakes it, reading a
pristine snapshot of the study's numbers so it can never compound.

That is eleven times the size of `legalWait`, which was the only previous change.

## The wind rule is dead, and three of the four were already answered

The old NEXT listed four cheap untested rules. **It was stale**: `two_discard_piles`,
`pair_discards_rule_out` and `second_copy_call` were all measured on 2026-09-03 and written up. Only
`wind_discard_order` was still open.

It is now measured on both populations over 110,000 wind discards and it is nothing. The wind of the
player before you is ponged 7.46% of the time against 7.38% for the player after you at a coach
table, 5.02% against 5.03% on the recorded run, with identical draws. The mechanism is where it
fails: the rule needs a wind's owner to want it, and the owner accounts for barely a third of the
pongs, which is exactly what three opponents claiming at random would give. `datagen/src/windorder.ts`
is the tool and the Tips card now says contradicted.

## The app has all five components now

The spotting drill is in, as the Spot tab. A position is shown behind a countdown of 3, 5 or 8
seconds, the whole thing goes face down, and then one of four questions is asked: how far from ready
the hand was, which suit it held most of, which opponent had the most sets face up, and which shape
the position was about. Scores are kept per question type, because that is the diagnosis.

`datagen/src/spotpack.ts` builds its 426KB pack. Two corrections are baked into that generator and
should survive any rebuild: the answer to the distance question is levelled by quota, because the
quiz pack runs late and half its positions are already ready, and a tagged position asks the shape
question half the time, because only two positions in five carry a tag.

## The tagger names nine tips now, and the block split is agreed

The old NEXT said `five_blocks`, `six_blocks_ok` and `narrow_can_beat_wide` could not be tagged.
Two of them already could, since 2026-09-03. The third, `six_blocks_ok`, is done on 2026-09-05:
`blocks` in `solver/src/shapetag.ts` splits a hand into named pieces with a fixed tie order, and the
block rule and its exception are one detector that fires as one card or the other, never both.
Play-outs say `six_blocks_ok` leans the book's way on 22 resolved positions and that is not a
finding. FINDINGS has the numbers.

Two tools to know about. `datagen/src/retag.ts` re-tags a pack in place in seconds, and it is how
the money pack got its tags at all. And the honest count is that tagging covers 840 of 4,147
discard positions in the coach pack, that a better split does not raise it, and that the only thing
that will is rebuilding the pack against the current tagger, because `quizpack.ts` fills each
stratum tagged-first.

## Where to start next, in order

**1. The 29 untested playbook rules.** The two cheap sub-claims are answered on 2026-09-05, on
both populations: the 2-or-8 exception to `pair_discards_rule_out` is wrong, and the narrow form of
`two_discard_piles` is real once "declined" means "could have claimed" - a tile that passed a seat
from the player on their left is a third to a half safer against that seat than one that passed
from anywhere else. FINDINGS has the tables. Neither is priced, and the standing rule about the
danger model still applies; the chow read is the first one to SHARPEN the on-the-floor discount
rather than restate it, so if that rule is ever revisited this is the read to try. What remains is
not cheap: read the badges on the Tips page.

**2. Decide whether to raise `OVERDRAW`, which is the real coverage lever.** The rebuild is done
and FINDINGS says what it bought: the money pack doubled to 796 tagged questions because it had
never been filled tagged-first, and the coach pack did not move at all, because every tagged
candidate was already being kept. So the tagger was never the constraint - the candidate draw is.
`OVERDRAW` in `quizpack.ts` is 2.5 and tagged questions scale with it almost exactly, so 5 gives
roughly 1,600 on the coach pack for about 50 minutes a pack instead of 25. The cost is not the time.
It would make a third of the Real Quiz be about a named shape against a sixth now, which is a
decision about what the quiz is for, and it is Changs's to make.

**3. The block split now exists, so the other block tips are within reach.** `linked_blocks`,
`sandwich`, `stepping_stones`, `perfect_one_away` and `sticky_one_away` are all claims about named
pieces, and every one of them is currently confirmed by counting on a hand we made up and never
scored against a play-out. Each needs its own detector, and `blocks` is the part they had in common.

**4. Still open, and a preference rather than a finding.** The old `run-money4` quiz pack and film
room are 14MB of tracked files. `run-money4` stays on disk either way, so they can be rebuilt in
half an hour.

## Habits that keep proving themselves

**Play it out before believing it.** Being right per-decision has failed to predict winning three
times. The only measure that counts is chips per game from `solver/src/tools/headtohead.ts`.

**Change the shuffle, and name the ranges before you launch.** Everything quoted above was measured
on ranges named in the message that started the run. Compare the `nocheap` batch that arrived after
its pool was assembled, which is recorded in FINDINGS and quoted from nowhere.

**Re-run any positive on a fresh named range.** `legalWait` fell from +0.048 to +0.018 that way. The
row correction did not fall: +0.190 then +0.210, which is why it shipped.

**Build the control at the same time as the arm.** The scale sweep spent a night and settled nothing
because every variant changed how decisive the coach was as well as what it believed. The row
correction was played beside a flat multiplier matched to its own decisiveness, so the two differ in
one thing only, and that control is what showed the real effect was larger than it looked.

**Say who is at the table.** The suit read, the honour wait and `triplet_adjacency` all split by
population. Measuring on one population is measuring half the question.

**Do not tune the danger model.** Five measured failures: the wall read, the suit read, the meld
read, the danger sweep, and the cost of a deal-in.

**Typecheck the web build, not just your own package.** It had been failing for weeks, so nothing
was deploying, and every session missed it by checking only the package it was working in.
